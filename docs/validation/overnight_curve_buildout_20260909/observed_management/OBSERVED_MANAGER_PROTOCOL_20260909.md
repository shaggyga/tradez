# Observed-quote management boundary

This new pure module prepares five virtual management decisions, then applies their accounting to the same independently observed current GBP/USD quote. It reuses the existing USD selection and valuation arithmetic without changing the prior replay, any forecast, or a registered cohort. It neither collects data nor places orders.

The five isolated arms are the unchanged legacy selector with the newly declared momentum input/settings, the USD manager with that momentum input, the same USD manager with the fixed curve, no trade, and the curve entry followed by holding without rotation. The latter remains subject to the common native target. This single-pair pilot can compare entry, hold, exit and reversal; it cannot establish cross-pair switching efficacy.

The experiment fixes a USD 2,500 research notional, integer base units sized at the decision quote, one position, a 5-bps entry-spread ceiling, 0.1-bps slippage on each leg, a cost ratio of 1 and a USD 0.05 switch hurdle. These are research assumptions, not the user's account or actual broker fills. The legacy reference additionally fixes a score-cost ratio of 1, a rotation multiple of 1.25, a maximum holding time of 60 minutes and a 3,600-second feedback horizon. Its selection code is reused; its new input/settings are not presented as a replay of a prior deployment.

One consumed initial official-midpoint curve is bound by its model, cohort, node, original price reference, source hashes, issue clock and expected terminal price. Its original H1 target never moves. Each later candidate uses the actual current quote to calculate the remaining signed price difference, including a legitimate zero continuation estimate. The original training endpoint window of nominal through seven seconds later is retained as an explicit approximation of the nominal management boundary. No remaining-horizon probability is fabricated.

The real controller exposed a compatibility defect at fractional decision clocks: the frozen curve adapter stores Python's floating-point `target - decision`, while the frozen replay compares against subtraction of the decimal representations of those epochs. The new boundary first validates the original adapter schema, seal and exactly that original floating-point subtraction, without a tolerance. It then creates a separately named and sealed `observed_curve_decimal_clock_compatibility_v1_20260909` payload with the decimal subtraction expected by the reader. The full original candidate and its seal, all timestamps, prices, target-window fields and probability scope remain unchanged in the retained plan. The compatibility payload has its own hash and normalizer source binding, rather than reusing the original candidate schema/seal. One-ULP changes to the original remaining time are rejected. Neither frozen source was edited.

## API and clocks

`validate_config(config)` validates the fixed contract. `initial_states(config, clock=...)` returns five flat states observed after contract creation and before the declared start. The caller is responsible for checking the registered source closure, original curve publication/consumption and actual source files; a checksum is not independent attestation.

`plan_actions(states, frame, config, clock=...)` accepts a frame containing scheduled and actual decision epochs, a terminal Boolean, raw quote bytes and their original receipt, at most one bound curve candidate, and optional original momentum raw/receipt/consumption evidence. It independently maps the quote and replays the momentum helper. A normal frame must lie on the declared 60-second grid, no more than ten seconds late. No catch-up decision or synthetic quote is inferred.

The frame's `decision_epoch` is the information cutoff. All five choices are calculated before the actual clock is sampled into `plan_created_epoch`. The caller must durably persist that sealed plan and provide the actual write start/completion through `make_plan_publication` before polling execution observations. Input availability does not claim earlier completion of derived computations.

A terminal frame is scheduled at the original target minus one second. It may explicitly contain `quote_raw=None`, `quote_receipt=None` and `decision_quote_refusal={"reason_code": "..."}`. That retained failure permits a price-independent mandatory exit decision. It does not supply a price or authorize a virtual exit without a subsequent valid quote. Missing decision quotes on normal frames are refused.

`settle_actions(states, plan, publication, observations, as_of_epoch=..., config=..., clock=...)` takes a bounded list of original quote bytes/receipts. The earliest eligible observation is chosen by actual read completion and receipt identity, never by its price. The read must start after plan publication and complete no earlier than the later of plan creation plus one second and publication completion. The action window lasts ten seconds. The quote must belong to the current generation, be explicitly tradeable and no more than five seconds old. A recent older market tick is retained with its original time; the new fact is its independently recorded later observation.

Entry requires the entire observation window to finish before the original target and plan completion at least twelve seconds before it. Terminal observations must also follow the target and arrive within fifteen seconds of it. Missing or invalid terminal quotes retain an unresolved position and an unavailable liquidation value. A flat cash balance remains known without a quote. An unsuccessful reversal is atomic and cannot silently close the incumbent first.

The settlement retains `as_of_epoch` as its source/information cutoff. Its actual clock is sampled after all five accounting calculations into `settlement_computed_epoch`; a completed next state's `known_epoch` equals that later clock. A pending result records computation completion without advancing the state. Completion cannot precede the information cutoff or exceed the registered hard stop, itself no later than 2026-09-09 13:00 UTC. The caller must persist the settlement/state and its actual publication receipt before making the next frame.

## Validation and limits

The final focused suite has 70 passing synthetic/temp-file tests, including quote ordering, publication latency, stale/future/non-tradeable observations, terminal missingness, computation clocks, native target preservation, zero continuation, policy drift, exact USD arithmetic, atomic reversal failure and the fractional-clock compatibility boundary. The earlier 38-, 55- and 60-case receipts remain retained. The first compatibility test run had 69 passes and one fixture failure: one nominal fractional clock rounded to an integer at this epoch magnitude, where the old reader correctly accepted it. The corrected test preserves that case and bases its expectation on the actual represented clock. No live episode, broker action, model refit or predictive improvement is claimed by this acceptance.

The caller must retain every attempted observation and cadence/refusal record; this pure boundary cannot prove that an omitted external observation never occurred. Observation batches have explicit count and byte ceilings. All arms share one market episode, so their decisions and P&L paths are dependent rather than independent trials. Hypothetical bid/ask accounting with declared slippage is distinct from an observed broker fill or a claim of available liquidity.
